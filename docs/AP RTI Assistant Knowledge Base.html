AP RTI Assistant now RTI + agriculture, English + Telugu

An assistant that answers ordinary people's questions about two areas of the Andhra Pradesh government: the Right to Information Act, and farm schemes, subsidies, crop insurance and agriculture rules. It uses only documents the government publishes, cites every source, and answers in English or Telugu.

What it does, on one real question

A citizen types the way people actually type. The assistant decides which documents the question is about, finds the passages that answer it, and answers only from those, naming the source it used.

how much money do i need to pay for an rti in andhra pradesh

Village-level public authority: no fee. Mandal-level: ₹5 per application. All other public authorities: ₹10 per application. Copies are ₹2 per page; a CD is ₹100. Inspecting records is free for the first hour, then ₹5 per hour. White-card (below-poverty-line) holders pay no application fee.

G.O.Ms.No. 454 (2005) G.O.Ms.No. 545 (2005) G.O.Ms.No. 740 (2007)

Checked against the orders themselves: every amount is correct, including the inspection fee as amended in 2005 rather than the original rate. This is Andhra Pradesh-specific detail that the state portal's own chatbot did not give.

rti fee entha (Telugu typed in English letters: "how much is the RTI fee")

Since 6 October the same question in Telugu, in either alphabet, is recognised as Telugu, translated to English for the search, and answered in Telugu script. Amounts, order numbers and section numbers stay exactly as the documents give them. Followed step by step in One question, now.

Three promises

  • Only the documents. Answers are written from retrieved passages, never from the model's own knowledge, and each claim carries a citation.
  • "I don't know" is allowed. Where the passages don't cover something, the answer says so. A question outside both topics is declined, not guessed.
  • Your language. Ask in English, Telugu script or Telugu in English letters; the answer comes back in the same language unless you choose another.

What it is not

  • Not legal or farming advice, and not a filing service.
  • Not current on everything: the indexed RTI orders stop at 2011 (newer ones are collected but not yet indexed), and several agriculture orders cover a single season.
  • Not ready to run unsupervised: 3 of 82 RTI and 13 of 93 agriculture test questions still miss their answer, and answer quality is checked by reading, not measured.

Why RTI wants words, not a reranker

RTI questions are abstract ("can they just say no") and the Act answers in legal phrasing ("no obligation to give any citizen"). A reranker trained on web searches can't connect the two, so it promotes passages that merely share surface words like "officer" and "information". What works is putting the Act's own concept names into the search: "third party information", "first appeal", "private body".

Why agriculture wants a reranker

Farmers name concrete things (cashew, flood damage, seeds, a cow, a drone) that sit inside long table chunks. A single embedding of the whole chunk blurs them; a cross-encoder reads question and chunk side by side and sees them. The LLM doesn't know AP's recent orders, so rewriting helps less here.

The method

  • Each test question carries a probe: a short phrase copied verbatim from the passage that answers it, e.g. senior in rank.
  • A question passes if its probe appears in the top 10 retrieved passages. No judging, no cost.
  • Probes are matched with spaces removed, so extraction damage (rup ees) doesn't cause false misses.
  • Every set was written and frozen before the changes it tests. Every LLM output is cached in eval/cache/, so the numbers reproduce without API calls.

Part 2 · unchanged since 29 September

The RTI foundation

How the RTI assistant was built and why: the problem, the documents, three questions traced through the search, and the decisions with their numbers. Still accurate as history; the figures here are September's.

Why the information exists but citizens can't use it

Everything a citizen needs is published on ap.gov.in. Two things stand in the way: how the site is organised, and the words the law uses.

The portal is a filing cabinet, not a guide

The "RTI & Acts" menu holds 546 documents. Most are there because Section 4(1)(b)(v) of the Act obliges every authority to publish "the rules, regulations, instructions, manuals and records held by it", so the menu fills with civil-service promotion rules and secretariat manuals.

  • The RTI Act itself is not in the "Acts" section. It sits four levels deep under Department – Org RTI → GAD → GOs / Governors Reports under s.4(1)(A), in a file titled just RTI ACT.
  • The one page written for citizens, "Instruction on RTI", lists How to apply · Fee payable · Time limit · Appeal Provision, and none of them link to anything.
  • A bug in the department section shows the previous department's contents when you open a second one, hiding 282 of its 285 files.

The words don't match

This is the part that breaks ordinary search. Citizens and statutes use different vocabulary for the same idea, and half the words a citizen reaches for never appear in the Act at all.

A citizen asksThe Act saysIn the Act?
How do I apply?shall make a request in writingdifferent word
They didn't replyshall be deemed to have refused the request"reply" never appears
Complain to the bossappeal to the officer senior in rank"boss" never appears
What's the deadline?within thirty days of the receipt of the request"deadline" never appears

Why that defeats a plain search, precisely

A search by meaning has no notion of which words matter. "How do I apply for information under the RTI Act?" shares 60% of its words with the passage that answers it, but every shared word is one the whole corpus uses.

Word in the questionChunks containing itWorth as a clue
information350 / 535  (46%)almost none
under288  (38%)none
act195  (25%)weak
rti46  (6%)some
apply6  (1%)the only real clue, and the answer doesn't contain it

The rule that explains nearly every result: retrieval works when the question's rare word also appears in the answer. "penalty" (2 chunks) and "refuse" (1 chunk) do, and those questions ranked 2nd and 3rd. "apply" does not, and its answer sat at rank 106 of 766.

This is a known problem, not a local one

A 2026 study of search over government benefit rules (The Vocabulary Gap Is an Equity Gap, arXiv 2609.01645) measured the same effect: keyword search found the right passage in its top 5 for 100% of formally worded questions but only 44% of plain-language ones. Plain questions shared 11% of their content words with the answer, against 63% for formal ones. The people who most need help are served worst.

Every one of the 546 documents in the portal's RTI & Acts menu was examined and classified. Most are irrelevant to a citizen; a large share of what remains is statistical filing.

Three layers, three kinds of question

The law: 1 document, 23 pages

The RTI Act 2005. Answers "what is my right": how to apply (s.6), fees and the 30-day limit (s.7), what can be refused (s.8), appeals (s.19), penalties (s.20).

AP's implementation: 26 orders, 2005–2011

Government orders: the state fee rules and their amendments, the Information Commission's constitution and appointments, compliance instructions.

Departments: 13 manuals + the portal page

Each department's own s.4(1)(b) manual: what it holds and who its Public Information Officer is. The portal page is the only source naming the current Commissioners.

Added in October: 44 agriculture documents

AP government orders (crop insurance, Annadata Sukhibhava, subsidies, MSP procurement, disaster relief), scheme guidelines, and central Acts on seeds, fertilisers and pesticides: 1,895 chunks in their own index. How they were chosen: ingest/incoming/agriculture/REPORT.md.

How they are cut up

  • The Act is split at its 31 section boundaries before chunking, so a chunk never straddles two sections. All 31 section titles were verified against the Act's own text.
  • Chunks are 350 tokens with 60 overlap, aligned to sentence ends, sized for the embedding model's 512-token window, so nothing is silently cut off. In the first version, 27% of chunks were being truncated.
  • Every chunk carries a citation label: "RTI Act 2005, Section 19: Appeal", or a department name, or "most current source" for the portal page.
  • Amendment links between orders are detected automatically at this stage, from each order's "Amendment to … Read the following" header. (Agriculture's come from a curated list instead.)

The HyDE passage that found it, which is itself wrong

It claims the Act does apply to intelligence agencies. That does not matter: it is only ever used to search. The answer, written from the real Section 24, says "No" and cites it. The passage is never shown to the answering model or to the user.

Glossary

Terms used above, in plain words: the machine-learning ones, the RTI ones and the agriculture ones. new marks terms added in October.

RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation: find relevant passages first, then have a language model answer using only those. It keeps answers tied to real documents.
Router new
One small LLM call before any search that decides which documents a question belongs to (RTI, agriculture, or neither) and what language it is in.
Chunk
A passage-sized piece of a document. Documents are too long to search whole, so they are cut into chunks of about 350 tokens: 535 for RTI, 1,895 for agriculture.
Embedding
A list of numbers representing a piece of text's meaning. Texts with similar meanings get similar numbers, so closeness can be measured.
BM25
Classic keyword search. It rewards rare words and ignores common ones, which is why it catches "penalty" or "court fee stamp" where meaning-search blurs them.
Hybrid search
Running meaning search and keyword search together and combining the results, because each catches what the other misses.
RRF
Reciprocal Rank Fusion: a standard way to merge several ranked lists. A passage ranked highly by any list rises overall.
HyDE
Hypothetical Document Embeddings: a small model writes the passage the documents would contain to answer the question, and that is searched too. It may be factually wrong; it is used only to find documents.
Topic + key terms new
An LLM names the rule or scheme the question is about ("Application fee for obtaining information") and lists words the answer likely contains. Both are searched alongside the question; neither is shown to anyone.
Reranker (cross-encoder) new
A model that reads the question and one passage together and scores how well they match. Sharper than comparing embeddings, but slower, so it re-checks only the top 30. Used for agriculture only.
Fused vs. replaced new
Merging the reranker's order with the search's order instead of trusting the reranker alone. Consistently safer: the reranker is a second opinion, not the judge.
Translation for the search new
A Telugu question is translated to English so the English documents and English models can be searched. The answer model still reads the original question.
Telugu in English letters new
Telugu typed with the English alphabet, the way people write in chat ("rti fee entha"). Also called romanized Telugu.
Reply language new
The language the answer is written in. By default the question's; the Reply in menu can set English, Telugu script, or Telugu in English letters.
Condense / follow-up rewrite
Turning a follow-up ("what if i still dont get it") into a standalone question before searching, using the last few turns.
Probe, top 10 new
A short verbatim phrase from the passage that answers a test question. A question "passes" when its probe is in the 10 passages the answer model receives.
Clean / final set new
Test questions written before an experiment and not used to choose anything. The final set is run only once, at the end, to confirm a gain.
Fallback new
When a Groq model hits its daily limit, the step switches to the next model in line (or is skipped), and the limited model is rested for 10 minutes.
Groq free tier new
The hosted LLM service used here: 200,000 tokens per model per day, and 8,000 tokens a minute on the answer model. About 40–45 full answers a day on gpt-oss-120b.
Chroma
The local database holding the chunks and their embeddings, one collection per topic. Searching happens on this computer; only the LLM calls go to Groq.
Docker image / container new
An image is a packaged copy of the app with everything it needs; a container is that image running. Here: one image, started with docker compose up.
Not in force new
A label on agriculture orders for schemes that were replaced (e.g. YSR Rythu Bharosa, now Annadata Sukhibhava). At most one such passage reaches the answer model.
PIO
Public Information Officer: the official in each department who receives and answers RTI applications.
First / second appeal
If a request is refused or ignored: first appeal to an officer senior to the PIO within 30 days; then a second appeal to the State Information Commission within 90 days.
Deemed refusal
No reply within the time limit counts as a refusal, so the appeal clock starts without waiting.
Section 4(1)(b)
The clause obliging every public authority to publish information about itself: the manuals that make up a third of the RTI corpus.
G.O.
Government Order: how the state issues instructions and rules, e.g. G.O.Ms.No. 454 setting the RTI fees.
White card
The AP ration card treated as proof of below-poverty-line status, which exempts the holder from the RTI application fee.
Annadata Sukhibhava new
AP's farmer income-support scheme, the 2024 name for what was YSR Rythu Bharosa. Several test questions ask about its amount, instalments and who is eligible (tenant farmers included).
