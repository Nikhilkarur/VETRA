What it does, on one real question

A citizen types the way people actually type. The assistant finds the passages that answer it in the AP government's own documents, and answers only from those, naming the section it used.

how much money do i need to pay for an rti in andhra pradesh

Village-level public authority — no fee. Mandal-level — ₹5 per application. All other public authorities — ₹10 per application. Copies are ₹2 per page; a CD is ₹100. Inspecting records is free for the first hour, then ₹5 per hour. White-card (below-poverty-line) holders pay no application fee.

G.O.Ms.No. 454 (2005) G.O.Ms.No. 545 (2005) G.O.Ms.No. 740 (2007)

Checked against the orders themselves: every amount is correct, including the inspection fee as amended in 2005 rather than the original rate. This is Andhra Pradesh-specific detail that the state portal's own chatbot did not give.

Two promises

  • Only the documents. Answers are written from retrieved passages, never from the model's own knowledge, and each claim carries a citation.
  • "I don't know" is allowed. Where the passages don't cover something, the answer says so. For a question about legal rights, a confident wrong answer is worse than none.

What it is not

  • Not legal advice, and not a filing service.
  • Not current on officials or amended fees: the state orders in the corpus stop at 2011.
  • Not reliable on one question in five, phrased casually. The open items are listed at the end.

Why the information exists but citizens can't use it

Everything a citizen needs is published on ap.gov.in. Two things stand in the way: how the site is organised, and the words the law uses.

The portal is a filing cabinet, not a guide

The "RTI & Acts" menu holds 546 documents. Most are there because Section 4(1)(b)(v) of the Act obliges every authority to publish "the rules, regulations, instructions, manuals and records held by it" — so the menu fills with civil-service promotion rules and secretariat manuals.

  • The RTI Act itself is not in the "Acts" section. It sits four levels deep under Department – Org RTI → GAD → GOs / Governors Reports under s.4(1)(A), in a file titled just RTI ACT.
  • The one page written for citizens, "Instruction on RTI", lists How to apply · Fee payable · Time limit · Appeal Provision — and none of them link to anything.
  • A bug in the department section shows the previous department's contents when you open a second one, hiding 282 of its 285 files.

The words don't match

This is the part that breaks ordinary search. Citizens and statutes use different vocabulary for the same idea, and half the words a citizen reaches for never appear in the Act at all.

Why that defeats a plain search, precisely

A search by meaning has no notion of which words matter. "How do I apply for information under the RTI Act?" shares 60% of its words with the passage that answers it — but every shared word is one the whole corpus uses.

Word in the questionChunks containing itWorth as a clue
information350 / 535  (46%)almost none
under288  (38%)none
act195  (25%)weak
rti46  (6%)some
apply6  (1%)the only real clue — and the answer doesn't contain it

The rule that explains nearly every result: retrieval works when the question's rare word also appears in the answer. "penalty" (2 chunks) and "refuse" (1 chunk) do, and those questions ranked 2nd and 3rd. "apply" does not, and its answer sat at rank 106 of 766.

This is a known problem, not a local one

A 2026 study of search over government benefit rules (The Vocabulary Gap Is an Equity Gap, arXiv 2609.01645) measured the same effect: keyword search found the right passage in its top 5 for 100% of formally worded questions but only 44% of plain-language ones. Plain questions shared 11% of their content words with the answer, against 63% for formal ones. The people who most need help are served worst.

Three layers, three kinds of question

The law — 1 document, 23 pages

The RTI Act 2005. Answers "what is my right": how to apply (s.6), fees and the 30-day limit (s.7), what can be refused (s.8), appeals (s.19), penalties (s.20).

AP's implementation — 26 orders, 2005–2011

Government orders: the state fee rules and their amendments, the Information Commission's constitution and appointments, compliance instructions.

Departments — 13 manuals + the portal page

Each department's own s.4(1)(b) manual: what it holds and who its Public Information Officer is. The portal page is the only source naming the current Commissioners.

How they are cut up

  • The Act is split at its 31 section boundaries before chunking, so a chunk never straddles two sections. All 31 section titles were verified against the Act's own text.
  • Chunks are 350 tokens with 60 overlap, aligned to sentence ends — sized for the embedding model's 512-token window, so nothing is silently cut off. In the first version, 27% of chunks were being truncated.
  • Every chunk carries a citation label: "RTI Act 2005, Section 19: Appeal", or a department name, or "most current source" for the portal page.
  • Amendment links between orders are detected automatically at this stage, from each order's "Amendment to … Read the following" header.

Three questions, followed end to end

Where the answering passage ranked at each stage, out of 535 chunks. Lower is better; anything past 10 never reaches the answering model. Two successes and one failure.

1 · "does rti work for intelligence agencies like IB" answered correctly

The answer is Section 24: the Act does not apply to the intelligence organisations in the Second Schedule. The citizen says "IB" and "work for"; the Act says "intelligence and security organisations" and "shall apply to".

meaning search, question 39
keyword search, question 6
both merged, question 14 — misses the top 10
meaning search, HyDE passage 2
keyword search, HyDE passage 2
everything merged 1

The HyDE passage that found it — which is itself wrong

"The provisions of the information law apply to all public authorities, including intelligence agencies, and any request … shall be processed in accordance with the statutory procedures, subject to the exemptions enumerated in the Act."

It claims the Act does apply to intelligence agencies. That does not matter: it is only ever used to search. The answer, written from the real Section 24, says "No" — and cites it. The passage is never shown to the answering model or to the user.

2 · "i sent my rti to the wrong office, will they forward it" answered correctly

Section 6 requires the office to transfer the application within five days. "Wrong office / forward" versus "held by another public authority / transfer".

question alone (plain RAG) 60
HyDE passage alone 2
both merged (what runs) 8 — inside the top 10

Merging is deliberately kept even though the passage alone scored better here. When a HyDE passage drifts off-topic, the original question is the safety net; across the test sets, merging ranked answers higher overall.

3 · "what if my family benglongs to bpl", asked after a fee question incomplete

A follow-up. The rewrite step turned it into "What if my family belongs to BPL?" — correct English, but it dropped fees, the thing the conversation was about.

AP white-card rule (G.O. 740) 43 — never retrieved
if the topic is carried through 1

The answer was still correct — it quoted the Act's own rule that no fee is charged to people below the poverty line — but it missed the AP-specific detail that a white card is what proves BPL status. Switching the rewrite to the larger model fixed this in an isolated test and not in the real conversation. It remains open; see open items.

The single most important line in that table

Better chunks, a better embedding model and keyword search took formal questions from 65% to 95% — and left casual questions at about 50%. Only HyDE moved those. Tuning ordinary RAG did not solve this corpus; bridging the vocabulary gap did.

How it is measured

One question, asked mechanically and without an LLM judge: does the text that answers the question appear in the passages the model was given?

The method

  • Each test question carries a probe — a short phrase copied verbatim from the passage that answers it, e.g. senior in rank.
  • A question passes if its probe appears in the retrieved passages. No judging, no cost, runs in seconds.
  • Probes are matched with spaces removed, so the corpus's extraction damage (rup ees, Informati on) doesn't cause false misses.
  • Every set was written and frozen before the changes it was used to test.

The measurement that pointed at retrieval in the first place

RAGAS, an LLM-judged evaluation, scored the first version 0.95 on faithfulness — the model stuck to what it was given — but only 0.38 on context precision, and 0.14 on "how do I apply". The model was trustworthy; it was being handed noise. That is why every subsequent change targeted retrieval, not the prompt.

Answers, checked by hand against the Act

Of five questions checked line by line: four correct (how to file, the fee schedule, what can be refused, the penalty) and one partly correct (a "no reply for 45 days" question that mixed up the first and second appeal). Saved in eval/demo_backup_answers.md.

Glossary

Terms used above, in plain words — both the machine-learning ones and the RTI ones.

RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation: find relevant passages first, then have a language model answer using only those. It keeps answers tied to real documents.
Chunk
A passage-sized piece of a document. Documents are too long to search whole, so they are cut into 535 chunks of about 350 tokens.
Embedding
A list of numbers representing a piece of text's meaning. Texts with similar meanings get similar numbers, so closeness can be measured.
BM25
Classic keyword search. It rewards rare words and ignores common ones — which is why it catches "penalty" or "court fee stamp" where meaning-search blurs them.
Hybrid search
Running meaning search and keyword search together and combining the results, because each catches what the other misses.
RRF
Reciprocal Rank Fusion: a standard way to merge two ranked lists. A passage ranked highly by either list rises overall.
HyDE
Hypothetical Document Embeddings: a small model writes the passage a law would contain to answer the question, and that is searched too. It may be factually wrong; it is used only to find documents.
Condense / rewrite
Turning a follow-up ("what if i still dont get it") into a standalone question before searching, using the last few turns.
Chroma
The local database holding the chunks and their embeddings. Nothing leaves the machine except the two calls that write the HyDE passage and the answer.
PIO
Public Information Officer — the official in each department who receives and answers RTI applications.
First / second appeal
If a request is refused or ignored: first appeal to an officer senior to the PIO within 30 days; then a second appeal to the State Information Commission within 90 days.
Deemed refusal
No reply within the time limit counts as a refusal, so the appeal clock starts without waiting.
Section 4(1)(b)
The clause obliging every public authority to publish information about itself — the manuals that make up a third of this corpus.
G.O.
Government Order — how the state issues instructions and rules, e.g. G.O.Ms.No. 454 setting the RTI fees.
White card
The AP ration card treated as proof of below-poverty-line status, which exempts the holder from the RTI application fee.

Run it

The documents and the index are already built and committed. Two commands from a fresh PowerShell.

The web page

cd C:\Users\nkk77\Desktop\ap-rti-assistant
python rag\web.py

Takes about 30 seconds to load, then opens http://127.0.0.1:8000. Citations are clickable and open the passage they came from; "How it searched" shows the HyDE passage. Ctrl+C stops it.

The terminal chat

python rag\chat.py

/sources shows the passages used, /new starts a fresh conversation, /quit exits.

Rebuild the index (only if documents change)

python ingest\load_and_chunk.py
python ingest\build_index.py

Re-run the measurements

python eval\retrieval_eval.py
python eval\retrieval_eval.py --no-hyde

The second command shows what HyDE is worth. eval\run_ragas.py runs the LLM-judged evaluation and needs extra packages.

Needs GROQ_API_KEY in a .env file at the project root. Leave about 30 seconds between questions: the free tier allows roughly 8,000 tokens a minute and one answer uses 3,000–4,000.

Open items

Stated plainly. Anyone demonstrating this should know them before someone else finds them.

One casual question in five still misses its answerSome misses are far off: for "can they refuse my RTI", the exemptions section isn't in the top 40.

Workaround: name the subject in the follow-up.

Throughput and startupAbout one answer per 30 seconds on the free tier; ~30 seconds to start, caused by a crowded global Python install rather than the pipeline. A project-only virtual environment would fix the latter.
English onlyTwo Telugu manuals are excluded; the embedding model is English-only.

Where it goes next

Most production systems close quality gaps by spending more per question — bigger models, rerankers, multi-agent pipelines. For a public service the cheaper route is to do the work once, at ingest. Two of the biggest wins here already cost nothing per question: splitting the Act by section, and linking amendments. The next experiment is to move HyDE's per-question call into indexing — generating, for each chunk, the questions it answers (the idea behind Doc2Query) — and to see whether quality holds without a per-question LLM call.
