Right to Information Act, 2005
The Right to Information Act, 2005 , is a crucial
law established by the
Parliament in 2005 under the democratic principles of
transparency, accountability , and
trust . It has provided citizens with a
path towards more active participation in a democratic country like India.
According to the RTI Act, any Indian citizen has the authority to request information
from an entity, which is accountable to furnish the requested details within a specified
timeframe. The legislation encompasses public
authorities and the
control of public authorities.
Key Objectives of Right to Information Act, 2005
Empowerment of Citizens
Grants citizens the right to access government information, enabling them to participate actively in democracy,
make informed decisions, and hold public officials accountable.
Transparency and Openness
Promotes transparency by reducing secrecy in government operations.
It enhances public oversight of governmental decisions and functioning.
Combat Corruption
Acts as a tool to expose corruption by allowing access to information on contracts, finances, and decisions—
ensuring accountability of those in power.
Features of the Right to Information Act, 2005
The RTI Act promotes transparency and accountability by empowering individuals to access information from public bodies.
Access to Information Through Legal Route
Citizens are legally entitled to request information held by government bodies, subject to certain exceptions under the Act.
Wide Coverage
Applies to all government departments , PSUs, and NGOs funded by the government .
Excludes national intelligence and security agencies .
Streamlined Application Process
Citizens may submit RTI requests physically or electronically . A nominal fee applies based on the type of data requested.
Time-bound Responses
Responses must be given within 30 days , or within 48 hours if it concerns life or liberty.
Appeals can be made to the First Appellate Authority (FAA) .
Significant Provisions of the Right to Information Act, 2005
Section 2(c) – Definition of Central Public Information Officer (CPIO)
The CPIO is an official designated by an agency under the RTI Act of 2005.
Their key responsibility is to support enforcement of the Act within their agency by receiving and processing information requests.
Section 2(h) – Definition of Public Authority
“Public authority” refers to any government organization or entity formed under the Indian Constitution, Parliament, State Legislature, or a government directive.
Section 4 – Right to Information & Obligations of Public Authorities
View details
This section outlines the responsibilities of public authorities to maintain records and proactively disclose information on their websites or platforms.
Government bodies must regularly communicate relevant information to the public.
Section 6 – Request for Obtaining Information
View how to request
Citizens can request information in Hindi, English, or the regional official language by submitting a written or digital application to the CPIO or SPIO.
A nominal fee must be paid with the request.
Section 8 – Grounds for Rejection of RTI Requests
View restricted information
Matters affecting the sovereignty & integrity of India
Security, strategic, scientific, or economic interests
Foreign relations and sensitive diplomatic information
Commercial confidentiality, trade secrets, and IP rights
Information that may endanger someone’s life or safety
Amendments to the RTI Act
The primary goal, behind amending the RTI Act was to exclude political parties from its jurisdiction. Consequently, Parliament decided to amend the act and introduced the RTI Amendment Bill, 2013 . Unfortunately, the bill did not progress as it lacked approval, from both houses of Parliament.
Then in 2019, there was another attempt in amending the Act through the RTI (Amendment Act) of 2019 which subsequently granted the Central Government the power to decide how long Information Commissioners can be in service, how much their salary and other benefits should be in monetary terms and other conditions of work.
RTI Amendment Bill, 2013
Proposed to exclude political parties from the scope of the RTI Act.
Introduced in Parliament but lacked approval from both houses.
Eventually, the bill lapsed due to lack of consensus.
RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019
Central Government to decide duration of Information Commissioners' service.
Authority over salary, allowances, and benefits of Commissioners.
Central Government empowered to determine all service conditions.
Information That Can Be Requested
The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005 empowers individuals in India to request access to information held by public authorities. This includes a wide range of categories such as:
Info on Government policies and decisions:
Individuals can seek information regarding government policies, decisions and the underlying reasoning behind them.
Government Initiatives:
Information pertaining to government programs, initiatives and their execution can be requested.
Spending and budgets:
Individuals have the right to inquire about public expenditure, budget allocations and how funds are utilized by government departments and agencies.
Records and official documents:
Access can be sought for official records, documents, reports, memos, emails and any other pertinent information held by public authorities.
Project specifics:
Details concerning public projects, contracts, tenders, agreements – including contractor details and beneficiaries – are open for inquiry.
Personnel related data:
Information on government officials such as their salaries, allowances, promotions, and transfers can be requested.
Safety and health matters:
Individuals can seek information on public safety protocols, health programs, sanitation facilities and other relevant public health issues.
Legal and judicial information:
You can also ask for details on cases and court decisions, rulings and legal viewpoints.
Frequently Asked Questions – RTI Act
1. What are the main points of the RTI Act?
Transparency, accountability, democracy and trust are the main points.
2. What is the Right to Information Act 2005?
The RTI Act, 2005 stands as a landmark law that grants individuals the right to
obtain information held by government entities.
3. In which case RTI can be rejected?
As per Section 8, RTI can be rejected if it comes under the sovereignty and
integrity of the nation as well as the strategic, security, or economic interests of
the state.
